Jabumarket started as a simple question: what would campus life feel like if the everyday things students need were easier to find?

On a typical campus, people are already buying food, finding services, selling items, looking for events, asking for help, and trying to move around with less stress. The problem is that all of this is scattered across WhatsApp groups, word of mouth, and random contacts saved with names you cannot remember two weeks later.

I wanted to build something that felt familiar enough for students to trust, but structured enough to make the experience better.

## The campus super-app idea

The long-term vision is a campus super-app: marketplace, services, deliveries, events, and local discovery in one place. Not because every product needs to become everything, but because student life is already connected. A food vendor, a delivery rider, a thrift seller, and a class rep may all be part of the same daily routine.

The first version does not need to be massive. It needs to be useful. That means starting with the pain that is easiest to understand: helping students discover what is available around them and helping sellers get seen.

## Why build it myself

I am self-taught, so every product is also a classroom. Jabumarket forces me to learn product thinking, databases, authentication, UI design, deployment, and the less glamorous parts of software that only show up when real people use something.

Building it in public keeps me honest. It gives me a record of the decisions, the wrong turns, the bugs, and the small improvements that compound over time.

## What I am learning

The biggest lesson so far is that good software is not just a stack. It is listening carefully, reducing friction, and making tradeoffs without pretending every feature belongs in version one.

Jabumarket is still early, but it already has a clear job: make campus commerce and discovery feel less chaotic.
