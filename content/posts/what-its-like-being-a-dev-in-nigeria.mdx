Being a developer in Nigeria can feel like carrying two jobs at once. There is the obvious work of learning, building, debugging, and shipping. Then there is the background work of staying steady through power issues, internet costs, currency stress, and the pressure to make things work quickly.

It is not impossible. It is just not always as clean as the tutorials make it look.

## Learning with uneven conditions

Some days, the biggest blocker is not JavaScript. It is data. It is electricity. It is trying to focus while life is loud around you. That kind of friction changes how you learn. You become practical. You download docs. You save videos. You learn to debug with limited time because you cannot assume the perfect setup will always be there.

There is a quiet resilience in that. It makes you resourceful.

## Building for your own environment

One advantage of being here is that the problems are close. You do not have to imagine friction in payments, logistics, education, small business, or campus life. You can see it. You can ask people directly. You can build from lived experience instead of guessing from far away.

That does not automatically make the work easy, but it gives it weight.

## The part I want to keep

I want to keep the discipline of showing up. Not perfectly, not loudly, but consistently. Write the post. Fix the bug. Push the small improvement. Ask the better question.

That is what gratitude-builds is for: a place to record the process while I grow into the kind of developer who can turn local problems into useful products.
